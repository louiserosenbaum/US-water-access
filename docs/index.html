Who drinks from wells in Minnesota?

Most of the state's community water systems pump groundwater, and most of them are small. Small systems are where health-based violations show up, and tribal systems show them most.

01

In most counties, it's mostly wells

In 74 of 87 counties with a community water system, at least half the people served get their water from groundwater systems. Statewide that's 68% of the 4,745,592 people these systems serve. The largest exceptions are Ramsey, St. Louis, Stearns and Clay counties, where the big systems draw from Lake Superior or rivers. Hover a county for its numbers.

Each system is placed in one home county. People on private wells are not counted here; this is community systems only.

02

Every system, by size

Each dot is one water system. Orange means at least one health-based violation in the last five years. 11% of systems under 500 people had one, compared with 4% of systems serving 10,000 or more. Diamonds are tribal systems, which EPA regulates directly.

Dots are spread up and down within each row so they don't sit on top of each other; the vertical position means nothing.

03

Among small systems, tribal systems had about 3.5x the violation rate

Comparing like with like (systems under 500 people), 7 of 21 tribal systems had a recorded health-based violation in the last five years, versus 46 of 477 state-regulated ones.

21 systems is a small group, so one or two systems moves the rate a lot. A recorded violation also depends on testing and reporting, so this measures what's on the record, not water quality directly.
