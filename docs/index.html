02

Three seas, one state

Rain that falls in Minnesota ends up in one of three places: the Gulf of Mexico by the Mississippi, Hudson Bay by the Red and Rainy rivers, or the Atlantic by Lake Superior. The divides meet at the Hill of Three Waters near Hibbing. Almost nothing flows in from other states; it all flows out.

By land area, about 57% of the state drains to the Gulf, 35% to Hudson Bay and 7% to the Atlantic.

Of the ~1.5 million people whose community system draws from a river or lake, 86% are on water headed for the Gulf, 9% for the Atlantic and 6% for Hudson Bay.

03

Every system, by size

Each dot is one water system. Orange means at least one health-based violation in the last five years. 11% of systems under 500 people had one, compared with 4% of systems serving 10,000 or more. Diamonds are tribal systems, which EPA regulates directly.

Dots are spread up and down within each row so they don't sit on top of each other; the vertical position means nothing.

04

Among small systems, tribal systems had about 3.5x the violation rate

Because small systems have more violations everywhere, this compares only systems under 500 people: 7 of 21 tribal systems had a recorded health-based violation in the last five years, versus 46 of 477 state-regulated ones.

21 systems is a small group, so one or two systems moves the rate a lot. A recorded violation also depends on testing and reporting, so this measures what's on the record, not water quality directly.

05

About 1 in 5 Minnesotans aren't on a community system at all

Subtracting the people community systems serve from each county's population leaves about 1,186,045 people (20%) who get water some other way, mostly private wells. Federal drinking water rules don't cover private wells, so testing is up to the owner. That estimate lines up with the Minnesota Department of Health's own figure of about 1.2 million private well users.

In 26 counties, at least half the people are off community systems. The highest shares are Cass (86%), Aitkin (79%) and Clearwater (77%). The most people are in Anoka County, about 88,418.

Estimate: Census 2025 county population minus people served by community systems. Some of these people are on small non-community systems, not private wells. Each system counts in one home county, so where systems serve across county lines the estimate is set to 0 (Cottonwood, Kittson, Lincoln, Ramsey). Tribal systems have no county in the EPA data and aren't subtracted.
